Review of Important Definitions

Let \(R\) be a ring.

  • A nonzero element \(a\) is a zero divisor if there is a nonzero \(b\) such that \(ab=0\).
  • A unit is an element with a multiplicative inverse.
  • An integral domain is a commutative ring with identity and no zero divisors.
  • A division ring is a ring with identity in which every nonzero element is a unit.
  • A field is a commutative division ring.

Proposition 16.1.10 - Subring Test

Let \(R\) be a ring and \(S\) a subset of \(R\). Then \(S\) is a subring of \(R\) if and only if the following conditions are satisfied.

  1. \(S\neq\emptyset\).
  2. \(rs\in S\) for all \(r,s\in S\).
  3. \(r-s\in S\) for all \(r,s\in S\).

Proof of 16.1.10

  • Choose \(s\in S\). Why does \(s-s\) show that \(0\in S\)?
  • For \(r\in S\), why is \(-r\in S\)?
  • How does subtraction closure imply addition closure?
  • Which remaining ring axioms are inherited from \(R\)?

\(\mathbb Z_p\)

Let \(p\) be a prime and consider the non-zero elements of \(\mathbb Z_p\), \(\{1,2, \ldots, p-1\}\).

Zero divisors? If we have the product of two non-zero elements \(a\cdot b=0\). Then \(p\) divides \(a\cdot b\), which means that \(p\) divides either \(a\) or \(b\), but \(p\ge a\) and \(p\ge b\) so this is not possible. So \(Z_p\) has no zero divisors.

This is not true for \(\mathbb Z_n\) is \(n\) is not prime.

\(\mathbb Z_p\)

Let \(p\) be a prime and consider the non-zero elements of \(\mathbb Z_p\), \(\{1,2, \ldots, p-1\}\).

Units? We also showed that the \(U(p)\) a cyclic group and any element other that 1 generates the group. This means that every nonzero element has an inverse under mulitplication. So \(Z_p\) is a field.

Example: Gaussian Integers

The Gaussian integers are

\[ \mathbb Z[i]=\{a+bi:a,b\in\mathbb Z\}, \]

where \(i^2=-1\).

  • \(\mathbb Z[i]\) is a subring of \(\mathbb C\).
  • Addition and multiplication are inherited from \(\mathbb C\).
  • The ring is commutative and has identity \(1\).

What kind of ring is \(\mathbb Z[i]\)? an integral domain? a field?

Multiplication in the Gaussian Integers

Let \(\alpha=a+bi\) and \(\beta=c+di\).

Then

\[ \alpha\beta=(ac-bd)+(ad+bc)i. \]

Since \(a,b,c,d\in\mathbb Z\), the product is again in \(\mathbb Z[i]\).

Also,

\[ (a+bi)-(c+di)=(a-c)+(b-d)i. \]

Therefore, \(\mathbb Z[i]\) is a subring of \(\mathbb C\) by the subring test.

Norm of a Gaussian Integer

For \(\alpha=a+bi\), define its conjugate by

\[ \overline{\alpha}=a-bi. \]

Then

\[ \alpha\overline{\alpha}=a^2+b^2. \]

We call \(N(\alpha)=a^2+b^2\) the norm of \(\alpha\).

  • \(N(\alpha)\) is a nonnegative integer.
  • \(N(\alpha)=0\) if and only if \(\alpha=0\).
  • \(N(\alpha\beta)=N(\alpha)N(\beta)\).

Units in the Gaussian Integers

Suppose \(\alpha=a+bi\) is a unit in \(\mathbb Z[i]\).

Then there exists \(\beta=c+di\) such that \(\alpha\beta=1\).

Taking norms gives

\[ 1=N(\alpha\beta)=N(\alpha)N(\beta). \]

Thus,

\[ 1=(a^2+b^2)(c^2+d^2). \]

Both factors are nonnegative integers.

What values can \(a^2+b^2\) have?

Units in \(\mathbb Z[i]\)

Since

\[ (a^2+b^2)(c^2+d^2)=1, \]

we must have \(a^2+b^2=1\).

The integer solutions are \((a,b)=(\pm1,0),\quad(0,\pm1)\).

Therefore, \(U(\mathbb Z[i])=\{1,-1,i,-i\}\)

The Gaussian integers are an integral domain but not a field.

Euclidean Domains: The Gaussian Integers

An integral domain \(D\) is Euclidean if it has a size function that allows division with remainder: for \(a,b\in D\), \(b\ne0\), there are \(q,r\in D\) such that

\[ a=bq+r,\qquad r=0\ \text{or}\ N(r)<N(b). \]

The Gaussian integers \(\mathbb{Z}[i]=\{a+bi:a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\}\) are Euclidean with norm

\[ N(a+bi)=a^2+b^2. \]

Given \(\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}[i]\) with \(\beta\ne0\), choose \(q\in\mathbb{Z}[i]\) by rounding the real and imaginary parts of \(\alpha/\beta\) to the nearest integers. Then \(r=\alpha-q\beta\) satisfies \(N(r)<N(\beta)\), giving the Euclidean division algorithm.

Quaternions

Let \(\mathbb H\) consist of elements of the form \(a+b\mathbf i+c\mathbf j+d\mathbf k\), where \(a,b,c,d\) are real numbers.

The ring \(\mathbb H\) is called the ring of quaternions.

  • Add by combining corresponding coefficients.
  • Multiply by distributing and using the relationships between \(\mathbf i\), \(\mathbf j\), and \(\mathbf k\).

Quaternion Multiplication

The generators satisfy the following relations:

  • \(\mathbf i^2=\mathbf j^2=\mathbf k^2=-1\).
  • \(\mathbf i\mathbf j=\mathbf k\).
  • \(\mathbf j\mathbf k=\mathbf i\).
  • \(\mathbf k\mathbf i=\mathbf j\).
  • \(\mathbf j\mathbf i=-\mathbf k\).
  • \(\mathbf k\mathbf j=-\mathbf i\).
  • \(\mathbf i\mathbf k=-\mathbf j\).

Note that this is noncommuntative.

Multiplicative Inverses in \(\mathbb H\)

Notice that

\[ (a+b\mathbf i+c\mathbf j+d\mathbf k) (a-b\mathbf i-c\mathbf j-d\mathbf k) =a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2. \]

This element can be zero only if \(a,b,c,d\) are all zero.

Thus, for \(q=a+b\mathbf i+c\mathbf j+d\mathbf k\neq0\),

\[ q^{-1}=\frac{a-b\mathbf i-c\mathbf j-d\mathbf k}{a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2}. \]

Hence \(\mathbb H\) is a noncommutative division ring.

Example 16.14: \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{2})\)

Consider

\[ \mathbb Q(\sqrt{2})= \{a+b\sqrt{2}:a,b\in\mathbb Q\}. \]

This is a subring of \(\mathbb R\).

To determine whether it is a field, consider a nonzero element \(a+b\sqrt{2}\).

Multiply by its conjugate:

\[ (a+b\sqrt{2})(a-b\sqrt{2})=a^2-2b^2. \]

Why can \(a^2-2b^2\) not be zero unless \(a=b=0\)?

Inverses in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{2})\)

For \(a+b\sqrt{2}\neq0\),

\[ (a+b\sqrt{2})^{-1} =\frac{a-b\sqrt{2}}{a^2-2b^2}. \]

Equivalently,

\[ \frac{a}{a^2-2b^2} -\frac{b}{a^2-2b^2}\sqrt{2}. \]

Both coefficients are rational.

Thus, every nonzero element has an inverse in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{2})\).

Therefore, \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{2})\) is a field.

Examples of Integral Domains and Fields

  • \(\mathbb Z\) is an integral domain but not a field.
  • \(\mathbb Q\), \(\mathbb R\), and \(\mathbb C\) are fields.
  • \(\mathbb Z_p\) is a field when \(p\) is prime.
  • \(\mathbb Z_n\) is not an integral domain when \(n\) is composite.
  • The quaternions \(\mathbb H\) form a division ring but not a field.
