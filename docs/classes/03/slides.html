Well Ordering of the Natural Numbers

A set \(S\) is well-ordered if every nonempty subset of \(S\) contains a least element.

Principle of Well-Ordering. Every nonempty subset of the natural numbers is well-ordered.

In this class the natural numbers are \(\mathbb{N}=\{0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots\}\).

What about the integers?

The integers \(\mathbb{Z}\) are not well-ordered.

For example,

\[ \{\ldots,-4,-3,-2,-1\} \]

has no least element.

Division Algorithm

Division Algorithm. Let \(a\) and \(b\) be integers, with \(b>0\). Then there exist unique integers \(q\) and \(r\) such that \(a=bq+r\) where \(0\leq r<b\).

The proof has three parts:

  1. Existence: Show that \(q\) and \(r\) exist.
  2. Verify \(0\leq r<b\)
  3. Uniqueness: Show that no other \(q\) and \(r\) can satisfy these conditions.

Integers Mod n

Equivalence Relation: Two integers \(a\) and \(b\) are equivalent mod \(n\) if \(n\) divides \(a-b\).

The integers mod \(n\) partition \(\mathbb Z\) into \(n\) different equivalence classes; we denote the set of these equivalence classes by \(\mathbb Z_n\). \(\mathbb Z_n=\{[0], [1], [2], \ldots, [n-1]\}\). Recall \([i]=\{s \in \mathbb Z : s=nl+i \text{ for some } l\}\).

Sometime we will get lazy and use \(\{ 0,1,\ldots,n-1 \}\)

Extending Addintion and Multiplication

For two integers \(a\) and \(b\), define addition modulo \(n\) to be \((a+b)\pmod n\); that is, the remainder when \(a+b\) is divided by \(n\).

Similarly, multiplication modulo \(n\) is defined as \((ab)\pmod n\), the remainder when \(ab\) is divided by \(n\).

Note on defining functions and operations on equivalence classes.

When defining functions or operations on equivalence classes we must verify that the are well defined. For example,

We must show that if

\[ [a]=[a'] \qquad \text{and} \qquad [b]=[b'], \]

then

\[ [a+b]=[a'+b']. \]

Checking

I’ll check the first argument of the operation; the second argument is the same. Assume \(a \sim a'\). I need to show that \(a+b \sim a'+b\).

Important

Whenever an operation is defined on equivalence classes, we must check that different representatives of the same classes give the same result.

What are the units?

Proposition 3.1.4.

Let \(\mathbb Z_n\) be the set of equivalence classes of the integers mod \(n\) and \(a,b,c \in \mathbb Z_n\).

  1. Addition and multiplication are commutative:

\[ \begin{aligned} a+b &\equiv b+a \pmod n \\ ab &\equiv ba \pmod n. \end{aligned} \]

  1. Addition and multiplication are associative:

\[ \begin{aligned} (a+b)+c &\equiv a+(b+c)\pmod n \\ (ab)c &\equiv a(bc)\pmod n. \end{aligned} \]

Proposition 3.1.4. Continued

  1. There are both additive and multiplicative identities:

\[ \begin{aligned} a+0 &\equiv a \pmod n \\ a\cdot 1 &\equiv a \pmod n. \end{aligned} \]

  1. Multiplication distributes over addition:

\[ a(b+c)\equiv ab+ac\pmod n. \]

Proposition 3.1.4. Continued More

  1. For every integer \(a\) there is an additive inverse \(-a\):

\[ a+(-a)\equiv 0\pmod n. \]

  1. Let \(a\) be a nonzero integer. Then \(\gcd(a,n)=1\) if and only if there exists a multiplicative inverse \(b\) for \(a\pmod n\); that is, a nonzero integer \(b\) such that

\[ ab\equiv 1\pmod n. \]
