Definition of a Group

A group \((G,\cdot)\) is a set \(G\) together with a law of composition

\((a,b)\mapsto a\cdot b\) satisfying:

  1. Associativity:
    \((a\cdot b)\cdot c=a\cdot(b\cdot c)\) for \(a,b,c\in G\).

  2. Identity:
    There exists \(e\in G\) such that \(e\cdot a=a\cdot e=a\) for every \(a\in G\).

  3. Inverses:
    For each \(a\in G\), there exists \(a^{-1}\in G\) such that \(a\cdot a^{-1}=a^{-1}\cdot a=e\).

Definition of a Subgroup

Let \(G\) be a group.

A subgroup \(H\) of \(G\) is a subset \(H\) of \(G\) such that when the group operation of \(G\) is restricted to \(H\), \(H\) is a group in its own right.

We write \(H \leq G\).

A subgroup uses the same operation as the original group.

Subgroups of \(\mathbb Z\)

Consider \(\mathbb Z\) under addition.

For \(n=0,1,2,\ldots\), define \(n\mathbb Z=\{nk:k\in\mathbb Z\}\).

Examples:

\[ 2\mathbb Z=\{\ldots,-4,-2,0,2,4,\ldots\} \]

\[ 3\mathbb Z=\{\ldots,-6,-3,0,3,6,\ldots\}. \]

For every \(n\geq 0\), \(n\mathbb Z \leq \mathbb Z\).

A Portion of the Subgroup Lattice of \(\mathbb{Z}\)

Every subgroup of \(\mathbb{Z}\) is of the form \(n\mathbb{Z}\) for some nonnegative integer \(n\).

Here \(m\mathbb{Z} \subseteq n\mathbb{Z}\) exactly when \(n\) divides \(m\).

Proposition 3.3.7 Subgroup Criterion

A subset \(H\) of \(G\) is a subgroup if and only if it satisfies the following conditions.

  1. Identity The identity \(e\) of \(G\) is in \(H\).

  2. Closure If \(h_1,h_2\in H\), then

    \[ h_1h_2\in H. \]

  3. Inverse If \(h\in H\), then

    \[ h^{-1}\in H. \]

Proposition 3.3.8 Another Subgroup Test

Let \(H\) be a subset of a group \(G\).

Then \(H\) is a subgroup of \(G\) if and only if

\[ H\neq\emptyset \]

and whenever \(g,h\in H\),

\[ gh^{-1}\in H. \]

Proof of Second Subgroup Test

One condition gives us everything:

  • Identity: \(gg^{-1}=e\in H\)

  • Inverse: \(eg^{-1}=g^{-1}\in H\)

  • Closure: \(h_1(h_2^{-1})^{-1}=h_1h_2\in H\)

Example of using the Subgroup Test

Let \(H=2\mathbb Z.\)

Since \(0\in H\), we know \(H\neq\emptyset\).

If \(a,b\in H\), then \(a=2m,\qquad b=2n\) for some \(m,n\in\mathbb Z\).

Therefore \(a-b=2m-2n=2(m-n)\in2\mathbb Z\).

Hence \(2\mathbb Z\leq\mathbb Z\).
